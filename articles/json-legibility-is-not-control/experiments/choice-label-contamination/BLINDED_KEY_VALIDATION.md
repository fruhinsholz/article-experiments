# Blinded semantic-equivalence screen

Date: 2026-09-27
Status: frozen before the refined Jev pilot

## Method

The four candidate triads were relabeled `K1` through `K4` and judged against the
same three category boundaries, without considering the expected Jev result:

1. every essential duty is possible at normal quality and pace;
2. some, but not every, essential duty is possible at normal quality and pace;
3. no essential duty is possible.

A triad passes only if its three labels preserve the full/partial/none ordering,
do not reverse a boundary, and remain plausible short choice names. The screen is
a semantic preflight, not evidence about Jev behavior.

## Blinded worksheet

| Blind ID | Slot 1 | Slot 2 | Slot 3 | Boundary preserved? | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K1 | `all_tasks` | `some_tasks` | `no_tasks` | Yes, when scoped by the question to essential work | Accept |
| K2 | `full_capacity` | `partial_capacity` | `no_capacity` | Yes, when scoped by the question to essential work | Accept |
| K3 | `fully_able` | `partly_able` | `unable` | No: `unable` is broader and more categorical than inability to complete essential duties | Reject |
| K4 | `complete_work` | `partial_work` | `no_work` | Yes, when scoped by the question to essential work | Accept |

Accepted confirmatory families: `task_coverage`, `capacity`, and
`work_completion`. The rejected `ability` family remains in the non-confirmatory
technical pilot solely to exercise the harness and must not be used to satisfy a
success criterion.

This screen was performed by the protocol curator. It is blinded to the desired
experimental outcome, but it is not an independent multi-rater human validation.
That limitation must remain visible in any article-facing account.
