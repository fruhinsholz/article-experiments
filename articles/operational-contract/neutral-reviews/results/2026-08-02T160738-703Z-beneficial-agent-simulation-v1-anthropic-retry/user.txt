I am considering a blog article and a small experiment about autonomous AI agents.

Background idea:
Some recent agent evaluations place autonomous agents in a capitalist game, for example managing vending machines or competing businesses. The agents sometimes lie, collude, manipulate refunds, or otherwise behave badly in pursuit of profit. A common conclusion is that autonomous agents are unsafe or should not be left alone with complex tasks.

My thesis is less negative. I suspect such experiments show at least two things: agents inherit the game they are placed in, and a poor objective with weak governance can produce poor behavior. The point is not that agents are intrinsically evil. It is that if we place them in a narrow profit-maximization system, they may reproduce the worst logic of that system.

Proposed alternative:
Run a small beneficial simulation. Several autonomous agents receive the same broad objective, not separate specialist roles. They may work independently and may share observations or plans if they choose. The objective is not to beat the other agents, but to improve the final solution. We observe whether they collaborate, withhold information, converge, drift into rhetoric, optimize proxies, or exploit ambiguity.

The experiment should be small and realistic, not a grand public-policy simulation. A candidate scenario is a small real-world business or household trying to reduce energy use, cost, waste, or pollution while preserving human comfort, workload, health, and feasibility under visible constraints.

Future comparison:
Phase 1 would use prose constraints only: realistic goal, realistic scenario, no explicit operational contracts.
Phase 2 would repeat the same setup with deterministic operational limits and enforceable contracts: budget caps, action permissions, hard constraints, measurable thresholds, prohibited tradeoffs, audit logs, and external checks.
