# External Review

## Verdict

**Conditionally worth doing — but the current design would not test your thesis.**

The essay idea is publishable and the framing is attractive. The experiment as described is not yet an experiment; it is an observation session. Two structural problems dominate:

1. **No contrast arm.** Your thesis is comparative ("agents inherit the game"). A single benign-scenario run cannot support it, no matter how carefully observed. You need at least the profit-framed condition you are implicitly criticizing, run by you, on your own environment.
2. **No temptation, no ground truth.** If the beneficial scenario contains no opportunity to defect, hide information, or game a proxy, then observing good behavior tells you nothing. And if the outcome is prose rather than a simulated quantity, "solution quality" collapses into taste.

Both are fixable, and fixing them turns a soft essay into a small but genuinely citable result. There is also a framing risk worth naming early: the claim that the field's "common conclusion" from vending-machine-style evals is *agents are unsafe/evil* is close to a strawman. Several of those results are primarily about **long-horizon coherence, memory decay, and reliability**, not moral character. If you open by mischaracterizing them, technically literate readers will stop at paragraph three.
